Privacy Policy

Effective: August 23, 2026 · Applies to the Staple iOS app.

Staple is a restaurant rating app made by an independent developer based in the United States. This page says what the app knows about you, in the order it finds out — the same account the app gives in Profile → Privacy, with the details a hosted policy owes you added.

The account you didn't make

Opening the app creates an anonymous account on the Staple server. It has no name, email or phone number attached — it exists so your ratings have somewhere to live. You are never asked to sign up, and everything in the app works without it.

What syncs

Your visits go to the Staple server: the place, the date, your two answers and the score they produce, your note, the tags you picked, who you said you were with, what you spent per person, and the dishes you called. So does your want-to-go list, with its notes and any links you saved to it, and the details of any place you add yourself — name, cuisine, price, address and its point on the map.

Your ratings live on your phone first and sync in the background, so logging works with no signal and catches up later.

Signing in with Apple

The app asks Apple for one thing: your email address, which you can hide behind Apple's relay if you prefer. Apple's identity is attached to the anonymous account you already had, so nothing you logged before signing in is lost. Until you sign in, your ratings live only on your phone; signing in is what lets them survive a reinstall or a new phone.

Who can see it

Nobody, until you claim a handle. Once you have one, anyone using the app who types it can find you — the search matches from the start of a handle, three characters or more, and returns nothing but the handle and display name.

Following is a request you accept or decline. People whose request you accepted see your ratings: the place, the date, the score, your note, your tags and your dish calls. They do not see what you spent or who you were with. When you and someone both follow each other, you each see the other's want-to-go list — the places, and the notes and links you saved with them.

There is no public feed and no leaderboard. Nothing you log is visible to anyone you have not accepted.

Blocking and reporting

Blocking someone stores one fact on the server — that you blocked them — so it can keep the two of you out of each other's feeds, searches, shared lists and plans. They are not told. Unblocking deletes that fact.

Reporting stores what you reported and the reason you picked, attached to your account, so the developer can act on it. Reports are never shown to the person reported, and nobody is told who reported them.

Plan invitations and notifications

If you use Plans and allow notifications, the app stores your device's push token on the Staple server so plan invitations and results can reach you. The token identifies your device to Apple's push service and nothing else; deleting your account deletes it.

Location

The app asks for your location in two places and nowhere else: the button on a map that centers it on you, and the "Use my current location" button when you are adding a place the app doesn't already have. If you have granted it, your location is also used to aim place searches at where you are. That happens on the phone. Your position is never sent to the Staple server — the only coordinates that leave the device are a place's own, including one you set from where you were standing.

Photos

Photos you attach stay on your phone. They are not uploaded, they are not in the feed your followers see, and they are not part of what comes back when you reinstall — deleting the app deletes them.

Importing from Google

The Takeout file you pick is read on your phone and never uploaded. The place names in it — up to fifty — are sent to the Staple server one at a time as searches, the same way typing them would be, so they can be matched against the places it already knows. It is a one-time snapshot, not a connection to your Google account.

No ads, no tracking, no selling

There are no ads, no analytics, and no third-party tracking or advertising code in the app. Your data is not sold, rented, or shared with anyone for marketing — there is nothing in the app that could. The only servers the app talks to are Staple's own (hosted on Supabase, in North America) and Apple's — for maps, place search, Sign in with Apple, and push notifications.

How long it's kept, and deleting everything

Everything above is kept until you delete it. "Delete account and data" at the bottom of Profile removes your ratings, want-to-go list, handle, follows, blocks and push tokens from the server immediately, and clears your phone. There is no undo, and you do not have to ask anyone to do it for you. Short-lived automatic server backups expire on their own within days of a deletion.

Children

Staple is not directed at children under 13, and the app does not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes

If what the app does with your data changes, this page changes with it, and the effective date above moves. The in-app Privacy screen is kept in step.
